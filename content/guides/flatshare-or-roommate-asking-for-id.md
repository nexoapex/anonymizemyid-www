You reply to a room advert. The response is quick and friendly, and before any mention of a viewing: *"Can you send a copy of your ID and a holding deposit so I can take the room off the market?"*

That is not referencing. That is the opening of a well-worn scam, and the sequence is what gives it away.

## The order is the signal

A genuine flatshare runs like this:

1. You reply to the ad.
2. You **view the room**, in person or over a live video call with the actual housemates.
3. You are offered it.
4. **Then** someone asks who you are, so names can go on an agreement.

Documents at step 4 are ordinary. Documents at step 1 are not, because at step 1 there is nothing yet to reference: no agreement, no tenancy, nothing that your identity is needed for. Anyone asking then wants the document itself.

## What the scam actually is

The listing is copied from a real letting somewhere else, with real photographs. The advertiser is abroad, or working away, or otherwise unable to meet — but will post the keys once the holding deposit clears. Two things get taken:

- **The money**, in a transfer that will not come back.
- **The identity document**, which is worth more than the deposit and is resold or used to open accounts. See [can someone steal your identity from an ID photo?]({{< relref "/guides/can-someone-steal-your-identity-from-a-photo-of-your-id.md" >}}).

Two checks cost you a minute each: **reverse-image-search the photos**, and **search the address**. Duplicated listings across several sites, or photos that belong to an estate agent's completed sale, end the conversation immediately.

| Stage | ID request | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before any viewing | Any | Red flag — decline |
| Before a viewing, with a deposit | Any | The scam, near-certainly |
| After viewing, before agreement | Name and photo | Normal — send a redacted copy |
| Agency referencing | May be broader | Ask which check, then decide |

## What to send when the request is real

Once you have seen the place and been offered it, a housemate or a small private landlord needs to know **who is signing**. That is your name and your photo. It is not your document number, your [machine-readable zone]({{< relref "/guides/what-is-the-mrz-machine-readable-zone.md" >}}), your date of birth or your signature — a live-in housemate is not running a statutory check and has no basis for keeping any of those.

So: cover those four, keep name and photo, and watermark it *"For [address] room share — [date]"*. [Anonymize my ID](/#get) does it on your phone with nothing uploaded. Where an agency is involved and a formal check genuinely applies, [when redacting your ID won't work]({{< relref "/guides/when-redacting-your-id-wont-work.md" >}}) sets out the boundary, and [is it safe to give a landlord a copy of your ID?]({{< relref "/guides/landlord-asking-for-id-or-passport-copy.md" >}}) covers the tenancy side.

## The sentence that settles it

> "Happy to send ID once I've seen the room and we've agreed — I don't send documents or deposits before a viewing."

A real advertiser says "of course". Everything else you needed to know arrives in the next message.
