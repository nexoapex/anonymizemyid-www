Redaction is the step everyone thinks of first, and rightly so. But redaction only decides what a copy *shows*. A watermark decides what that copy is **worth to whoever ends up holding it** — and it is the difference between a document that can quietly be reused somewhere else and one that obviously cannot.

## What a watermark actually does

It does not hide anything. Its job is to bind the copy to a single context. A passport copy with your name and photo visible is still, in principle, usable by someone else to support a claim about who they are. The same copy with *"For Acme Ltd onboarding — 28 July 2026"* repeated across it is:

- **Obviously out of place** anywhere except Acme's file,
- **Hard to present as an original** to a third party,
- **Traceable** back to the request it was made for, if it ever surfaces in a breach.

That is a lot of protection for something that takes ten seconds.

## What to write

Three elements, and all three matter:

| Element | Why it matters | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The recipient** | Ties the copy to one organisation | `For Hotel Marina` |
| **The purpose** | Stops reuse for a different check | `check-in only` |
| **The date** | Makes a stale copy obvious | `4 August 2026` |

Put together: *"For Hotel Marina check-in only — 4 August 2026."* Compare that to a plain `COPY` stamp, which tells a future holder nothing and constrains nothing.

## Where to put it

**Across the whole image, repeated.** This is the part people most often get wrong. A watermark placed neatly in one corner is removed by a single crop, and the cropped result looks entirely clean. Text tiled diagonally across the full frame cannot be taken out without visibly destroying the document underneath — which is exactly the property you want.

Keep the opacity in a workable middle: solid enough to read at a glance, light enough that your name and photo are still legible. The recipient does still have to complete their check, and a copy they cannot read is a copy they will ask you to send again.

## Watermark *and* redact — they are not alternatives

These two steps answer different questions:

- **Redaction** answers *what data does this copy expose?* — it removes the document number, [machine-readable zone]({{< relref "/guides/what-is-the-mrz-machine-readable-zone.md" >}}), date of birth and signature.
- **Watermarking** answers *what can be done with this copy?* — it constrains reuse of whatever remains visible.

A watermarked but unredacted copy still hands over the fields that matter. A redacted but unwatermarked copy is safer, but can still be recycled into a different context. Do both, in that order — see [how to redact a passport or ID card]({{< relref "/guides/how-to-redact-a-passport-or-id.md" >}}) for the redaction half.
