You list a sofa, a phone, a coat. A buyer messages, friendly and fast, and somewhere in the third or fourth message: *"Can you send a photo of your ID? Just for verification before I pay."*

The answer is no, and it is worth understanding why it is always no.

## Nothing in a private sale needs your document

Work through what actually has to happen for the sale to complete:

- **Payment.** The buyer sends money, or the platform's payment feature holds it. Neither step involves your identity document.
- **Collection or postage.** They need a place and a time, or an address on a label. Not a passport.
- **Trust.** Reasonable, and this is what the platform's ratings, its payment protection and its in-app chat exist for.

There is no fourth thing. Where a marketplace verifies its users at all — and platforms like Vinted do have genuine seller-verification obligations once your sales pass certain thresholds — it does so **inside the app, in a flow you open from your own account settings.** No verification system anywhere routes a document through the other party to the deal.

## The two things that follow

**A fake payment or courier page.** You are sent a link that looks like a carrier or a payment provider, "confirming" a payment that is waiting for you. It asks for a document photo and, a step later, card details. No courier and no payment provider asks a *seller* for a passport.

**An account opened in your name.** A clear photo of a data page carries your document number, date of birth, signature and [machine-readable zone]({{< relref "/guides/what-is-the-mrz-machine-readable-zone.md" >}}) — which is most of what an application for credit needs. See [can someone steal your identity from an ID photo?]({{< relref "/guides/can-someone-steal-your-identity-from-a-photo-of-your-id.md" >}}).

## The tell that comes first

Almost always, the ID request arrives **just after an attempt to move you off the platform**: "message me on WhatsApp, it's easier." That is not convenience. In the platform's chat there is a record, a report button and a moderation trail; on WhatsApp there is none of that, and the account can be deleted by the time you look for it.

Staying in the platform's own chat is the single most effective thing you can do, and it costs you nothing.

| What they ask | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Send ID to verify" | No such platform flow exists | Decline, report |
| "Message me on WhatsApp" | Removing the record | Stay in the app |
| "Confirm on this courier link" | Phishing page | Do not open it |
| "The payment is held until you verify" | Fake payment-protection page | Check your own account instead |
| "I'll send extra, refund the difference" | Overpayment scam | Stop; block |

## What to say

> "I don't share ID for private sales. Happy to complete this through the platform's own payment and delivery."

Then report the account. Marketplaces act on this pattern, and reporting is what makes the next seller's version of this conversation shorter.

## If a copy is genuinely needed for something else

Private sales are not the only time someone asks. When the request is real but informal — a host, a landlord, a client — a **redacted, watermarked copy** is the right answer: cover the document number, the MRZ, your date of birth and your signature, and stamp it with who it is for. [Anonymize my ID](/#get) does this on your phone with nothing uploaded.
