Most advice about redacting ID documents is written as if it always helps. It does not, and knowing exactly where the line falls is more useful than any redaction technique.

## The four cases where it does not work

**1. Banks and anything else under anti-money-laundering rules.** Opening or keeping an account, a payment institution, a crypto exchange, an insurer, a notary. The obligation to capture the complete document sits on the institution; Germany writes it out explicitly in § 8 of the Geldwäschegesetz. See [you cannot redact your ID for a bank]({{< relref "/guides/bank-account-id-verification.md" >}}).

**2. Guest registration at a hotel.** In Spain, Italy, Portugal, Greece and much of Europe the property must file your document number and date of birth with the authorities. Those are precisely the fields the usual advice tells you to cover. See [hotel wants a passport copy]({{< relref "/guides/hotel-asking-for-passport-copy.md" >}}).

**3. Statutory right-to-work and payroll checks.** A UK manual right-to-work check requires a copy showing exactly what the employer saw, including the date of birth, expiry and signature. A Spanish employer needs your DNI or NIE number to register you with Social Security. See [new employer wants your ID]({{< relref "/guides/employer-asking-for-id-or-passport-copy.md" >}}).

**4. Government procedures.** Visas, residence permits, tax registration, licensing. The authority is the issuer of the identity system; there is nothing to hide from it.

In all four, a black bar is not a privacy setting. It is a missing field, the check is rejected, and you send the full document a week later anyway.

## The much longer list where it does work

Everything with no regulator behind it — which is most of what actually lands in your inbox:

- A **marketplace buyer** who wants "verification" before paying.
- A **private landlord or letting agent** doing informal referencing.
- A **short-term rental host** messaging you outside the platform.
- A **client or agency** onboarding a freelancer.
- A **platform's support desk** recovering an account.
- A **gym, club, coworking space, storage unit or delivery point**.
- Anyone at all in a **WhatsApp, Telegram or Discord** conversation.

In every one of these the person asking has **no legal claim to your document number**, no obligation to capture it, and usually no retention policy for it either. Covering the number, the [machine-readable zone]({{< relref "/guides/what-is-the-mrz-machine-readable-zone.md" >}}), your date of birth and your signature costs them nothing they were entitled to.

## The one question that sorts any request

> **Is a law telling *them* to capture this document — or is this someone asking because it is convenient?**

| | Law obliges the recipient | Someone is just asking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Examples** | Bank KYC, hotel register, right-to-work | Marketplace, host, client, landlord, support desk |
| **Redact?** | No — the check fails | Yes |
| **Watermark?** | No — can read as tampering | Yes, name the recipient and date |
| **What protects you** | Using the official channel you started | Covering the fields and limiting reuse |
| **How common?** | A handful of times in your life | Several times a year |

## And when the answer is "no law, but they insist"

That is not a redaction problem, it is a negotiation. Ask what they need to verify, offer the redacted copy, and note that a refusal to accept one from a party with no obligation is itself informative — see [can a company legally keep a copy of your ID]({{< relref "/guides/can-a-company-keep-a-copy-of-your-id.md" >}}) for the GDPR footing, and [is it safe to send your ID over WhatsApp or email?]({{< relref "/guides/is-it-safe-to-send-id-over-whatsapp-or-email.md" >}}) for the channel.
