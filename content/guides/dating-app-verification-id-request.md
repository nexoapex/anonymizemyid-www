It starts well. The conversation is easy, they seem keen to meet, and then: *"Before we meet, could you get verified on this site? I've done it — it's just a safety thing."*

There is no such site. There has never been such a site. The category itself is the fraud.

## Why "cross-app verification" cannot exist

Think about what such a service would need: an agreement with every dating app to write badges into their profiles, and a reason for you to trust it with a document. Neither exists. The apps run their own verification precisely because they are the only ones who can attach the result to a profile.

So every page that presents itself as one is doing something else:

- **Taking card details** for a "free check" that is a recurring subscription buried in the small print.
- **Harvesting logins** with a page that imitates a real app's sign-in.
- **Collecting identity documents**, which are worth more than either.

## How real verification works

On the major apps, verification is:

1. **In your own settings**, opened by you, on the app you are already using.
2. **Optional.**
3. Usually a **live video selfie**, matched against your existing profile photos — a document is often not involved at all.
4. **Invisible to your match**, who sees a badge and nothing else. They never see your face-capture and never see a document.

That last point is the one to hold on to. There is no legitimate flow in which your match ends up holding anything of yours.

| What happens | Real verification | The scam |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where it starts | Your own app settings | A link from a match |
| Who asks | The app | A person |
| What it takes | Usually a live selfie | Document, card details, or both |
| What the match sees | A badge | Whatever you sent them |
| Cost | Free | A "free" check that bills |

## If it is not about verification, what is it about?

Two things, usually. A **romance scam** needs to establish that you follow instructions, and this is an early, low-cost test of exactly that. And a full identity document is a saleable asset on its own — enough to open accounts or pass someone else's check, as set out in [is it safe to send a selfie holding your ID?]({{< relref "/guides/selfie-holding-your-id.md" >}}) and [can someone steal your identity from an ID photo?]({{< relref "/guides/can-someone-steal-your-identity-from-a-photo-of-your-id.md" >}}).

## What actually makes meeting someone safer

None of it involves documents:

- **Video-call before meeting.** It defeats a stolen-photo profile in thirty seconds.
- **Meet somewhere public**, and get yourself there and back.
- **Tell a friend** where you are going and when.
- **Reverse-image-search their photos** — stolen profile pictures usually show up elsewhere.
