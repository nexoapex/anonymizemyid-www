Short-term rentals collapse two things people usually keep separate: a **platform** with verification systems and support, and an **individual host** with a phone and a chat window. When the ID request arrives, it matters a great deal which of those two is actually asking.

## The platform has already done this

Airbnb and comparable platforms run identity verification as part of the account, precisely because handing documents around between strangers does not scale safely. When that check happens, the document goes into the platform's system — not into a person's camera roll.

So when a host asks for a passport photo **in the chat**, they are not completing that check. They are opening a second, parallel one with none of the same handling: no retention policy, no deletion process, no support channel if it goes wrong. The copy lands in a private inbox and then behaves like any other photo — [backed up, synced, forwarded]({{< relref "/guides/is-it-safe-to-send-id-over-whatsapp-or-email.md" >}}).

## But the request is often legitimate

It is worth being fair about this: in several countries, short-term rental hosts carry the **same guest-registration duty as hotels**. In Spain, [Royal Decree 933/2021](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2021/10/26/933) requires accommodation providers — including private rentals — to collect specified guest details and report them to the authorities. A host asking for identity details before your stay may simply be complying with that.

What the duty requires, though, is that they **record certain details**, not that they keep a permanent photograph of your document. Which is exactly the distinction that lets you cooperate fully and still protect yourself — the same one covered in [is it safe to give a hotel a copy of your passport]({{< relref "/guides/hotel-asking-for-passport-copy.md" >}}).

## What a host actually needs

- Your **full name**, matching the booking.
- Your **nationality** and **document type**, where registration rules require them.
- Your **photo**, if they need to confirm the guest matches the reservation.

They do not need your document number, your [machine-readable zone]({{< relref "/guides/what-is-the-mrz-machine-readable-zone.md" >}}), your date of birth or your signature sitting in a chat thread after your stay has ended.

## Two rules that cover almost every case

**Keep it on the platform.** Send whatever you send through the platform's own messaging, never over WhatsApp, email or a link a host sends you. If something goes wrong, a conversation the platform can see is the difference between a support case and your word against theirs.

**Send a redacted, watermarked copy.** Cover the number, machine-readable zone, date of birth and signature; keep name, photo and nationality; and watermark it *"For [host or listing name], [your dates]"*. It satisfies a registration requirement and is worth nothing to anyone else afterwards.

## When to slow down

A few patterns deserve more caution than the request itself:

- Being asked for ID **before a booking is confirmed**, especially alongside a request to pay outside the platform.
- Being pushed to a **side channel** — "just WhatsApp it to me" — when the platform's messaging works fine.
- A request for a **second document** or additional details after you have already sent one.

Those are the shapes that rental scams take, and none of them are made safer by a document being genuine.
