Your account is gone. The email on it has been changed, the phone number too, and the only route left in the help pages ends with: *"Upload a photo of your identity document."*

This one is genuinely real, which makes it unusual on this site — and makes the fake version unusually effective.

## Why recovery is the exception

Most of the requests in these guides come from someone who could have asked for less. Account recovery is different: when a takeover has replaced every other proof you had, an identity document is one of the few remaining ways to show that the account is yours. The platform is not being lazy. It is out of options.

So the question is not *whether* to send one. It is **which flow you are in**.

## The one rule: you start it

| Real recovery | The fake |
| --- | --- |
| You open the app or type the help URL yourself | It reaches you — DM, email, comment |
| Runs on the platform's own domain | A lookalike domain or a form service |
| Free | A fee, often in crypto or gift cards |
| No human negotiates with you | Someone very helpful, very fast |
| Asks once, through the form | Asks again, then for more |

Everything that arrives *at* you is the scam. That includes the accounts that appear in your replies within minutes of you posting that you have been hacked — they are watching for exactly that post, and they are offering the second robbery to the person who has just had the first.

## What to send

Recovery compares **a name and a face** against the account. That is all it does with the document, so:

- **Cover the document number.**
- **Cover the [machine-readable zone]({{< relref "/guides/what-is-the-mrz-machine-readable-zone.md" >}}).**
- **Cover your signature.**
- **Keep your name and photo visible.**
- **Watermark it** *"For [platform] account recovery — [date]"*.

The watermark earns its place here more than almost anywhere: this goes into a support queue, is seen by people you will never identify, and may be retained under a policy you have not read. A copy that names its single purpose cannot be quietly reused. [Anonymize my ID](/#get) prepares it on your phone with no upload.

This is squarely on the redaction side of the line drawn in [when redacting your ID won't work]({{< relref "/guides/when-redacting-your-id-wont-work.md" >}}) — a support desk has no statutory duty to capture a complete document.

## Do the rest of the recovery too

The document is one step. The rest matters more:

1. **Secure the email account first.** It is the key to everything else, and it is usually taken before the social account is.
2. **Turn on an authenticator app**, not SMS, wherever it is offered.
3. **Check what else used that account to log in** — every "sign in with" connection is a second door.
4. **Tell your contacts**, because a taken-over account's next job is messaging them.

If a full copy of your document went out during any of this, work through [you already sent an unredacted ID copy]({{< relref "/guides/already-sent-an-unredacted-id-copy.md" >}}), and see [can someone steal your identity from an ID photo?]({{< relref "/guides/can-someone-steal-your-identity-from-a-photo-of-your-id.md" >}}) for what that actually exposes.
