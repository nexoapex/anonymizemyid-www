Onboarding a new client, and buried in the vendor pack: *"Please attach a copy of your passport or national ID."* It is a normal enough request. It is also one where almost nothing they need is actually on the document.

## What a client is really confirming

That the person sending the invoice is the person they hired. That is your **name** and your **photo**, and it is the whole of it.

What they usually think they need, but do not:

- **Your document number.** Nothing in a supplier record is keyed on it.
- **The [machine-readable zone]({{< relref "/guides/what-is-the-mrz-machine-readable-zone.md" >}}).** It re-encodes what is already legible.
- **Your date of birth.** Not part of paying an invoice.
- **Your signature.** Your contract already carries one, where it belongs.

And the thing they *do* need for the money to move — your **tax or VAT number** — is on your invoice, not on your passport.

Crucially, a client is **not a regulated institution**. No law obliges them to capture the complete document, which is the whole test set out in [when redacting your ID won't work]({{< relref "/guides/when-redacting-your-id-wont-work.md" >}}). So a redacted, watermarked copy is not a compromise here — it is simply the correct amount.

## The version to refuse

The same request has a fraudulent twin, and it is common enough in remote hiring to be worth naming:

| Real client onboarding | The recruitment scam |
| --- | --- |
| Comes after an offer or contract | Comes before anything |
| From a company domain you can check | Gmail, or a lookalike domain |
| After a call or video interview | Chat only, often on Telegram |
| Asks for tax details for invoicing | Asks for bank details "for payroll" |
| Will accept a redacted copy | Insists on the full document, urgently |

If there has been no contract, no call and no company that survives thirty seconds of searching, there is no job. The document and the bank details are the product.

## What to send when it is real

Cover the four fields above, keep name and photo, and watermark it *"For [Client] supplier onboarding — [date]"*. That last part matters more for clients than for most recipients: vendor records get exported to new systems, shared with accounts teams and inherited by whoever takes over the account. A copy that names its purpose is visibly out of place anywhere else — see [how to watermark a copy of your passport or ID]({{< relref "/guides/how-to-watermark-a-copy-of-your-id.md" >}}).

## Ask what happens to it afterwards

A supplier record has no natural expiry, so a document copy in one can sit there for years after the last invoice. Asking for the retention period, and asking for deletion once the engagement ends, is an ordinary request with a clear basis behind it — [can a company legally keep a copy of your ID]({{< relref "/guides/can-a-company-keep-a-copy-of-your-id.md" >}}) sets out how to put it.
